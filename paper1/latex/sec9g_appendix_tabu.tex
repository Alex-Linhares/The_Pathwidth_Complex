\section{A tabu search over minors, and its certificates}
\label{app:tabu}

The minor step of Section~\ref{sec:minor} builds one contracted copy of the
graph per size by a greedy rule and tests it. On the classes of the dataset
that it leaves open, the greedy minor typically certifies one less than the
recorded width: the contraction has cost one unit of pathwidth, and the
obstruction of the full width, if the recorded width is the truth, was crushed
by one of its merges. The ladder never varies the minor. This appendix
describes a search that does, states what it certifies, and proves the
certificates sound in Lean~\leanref{tabu}. The program is
\path{pathwidth_solver/cli/minor_tabu.c}, built on the same engine as the
solver.

\paragraph{The search.} The state is a set $F$ of edges of $G$ forming a
forest; its components are the branch sets of a minor $H=G/F$, the graph on
the components with an edge between two of them whenever $G$ has one. A move
adds an edge of $G$ joining two different components, one more contraction,
or removes an edge of $F$, one contraction undone, after which the component
splits into two connected halves. The search is a tabu search in the plain
sense: at every step every neighbour is evaluated, or a random sample of a
fixed size when there are too many, the best non-tabu neighbour is taken, the
reverse of the last seven moves is tabu, and a move that certifies the target
width is always allowed. The walk is kept inside a band of minor sizes around
its starting point, since a minor that keeps too few vertices has lost the
obstruction and one that keeps too many cannot be decided within the budget.
Two starting points are used: the greedy rung of the ladder, or, on an open
class, the best minor certificate on record, from which the walk explores
alternatives to the greedy contractions at that size.

A neighbour is scored by what it certifies. First, whether our corrected
algorithm refutes the target width $w-1$ on it within a small node budget,
which is the event that closes the class; then, whether it still refutes the
width the floor rests on, which says the known obstruction survived the move;
then its contraction degeneracy; ties are broken at random within the band,
with no preference for a size, and the program writes every minor on which a
neighbour refuted either level to a log, as a contraction sequence in the
certificate format below with the level and the step, so each such minor can
be recovered and re-checked afterwards and where certificates occur in the
band is measured rather than assumed. The chosen minor is then climbed
by the engine under a larger budget, each refutation of a width $v$ raising
the floor to $v+1$. The floor only rises, and the best certificate found is
what the program reports.

\paragraph{The certificate.} The output is the floor, how it was obtained,
the minor's size, and a contraction sequence: the forest's edges are walked
with a union-find, and for every edge whose ends are still in different
components the pair (representative of one end, representative of the other)
is emitted, meaning ``contract the first into the second''. Every pair is an
edge of the contracted graph at the moment it is emitted, so the sequence is
a contraction sequence in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:minor} and the
checker of Section~\ref{sec:certs} replays it. The floor is of one of two
kinds: \emph{refutation}, the engine refuted width $w$ on the minor, so the
floor is $w+1$; or \emph{contraction degeneracy}, the minor step's bound on
the minor.

\paragraph{What is proved.} The Lean development models the walk and the
certificate exactly (\path{Search/MinorTabu.lean}, 45 theorems, no
\texttt{sorry}, no axiom beyond the three standard ones). Write $r$ for the
representative map the union-find maintains, idempotent on $V$; merging an
edge $pq$ replaces $r$ by $r'$ with $r'(x)=r(q)$ when $r(x)=r(p)$ and $r(x)$
otherwise; the quotient $G/r$ is the graph on the representatives with an
edge between $r(p)$ and $r(q)$ whenever $pq\in E$ and $r(p)\neq r(q)$.

\begin{theorem}[the emitted sequence is a legal contraction sequence, and
replays to the quotient]\label{thm:tabu-seq}
For any list of edges of $G$ in any order, the sequence emitted by the walk
is a contraction sequence of $G$ in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:minor},
and applying it to $G$ yields $G/r$ for the final representative map $r$,
restricted to the surviving representatives. In particular
$\pw(G/F)\le\pw(G)$~\leanref{tabu}.
\end{theorem}

The proof rests on one identity that needs no edge at all: contracting
$r(p)$ into $r(q)$ in $G/r$ is the same graph as $G/r'$ for the merged map
$r'$, so one step of the walk is one contraction; the edge $pq$ only makes
the step legal. Acyclicity of $F$ is defined and never used: it is the
program's bookkeeping, by which an addition shrinks the minor by exactly one
vertex, and the theorems hold for any list of edges, cyclic or not.

\begin{theorem}[both floors are sound]\label{thm:tabu-floor}
Let $F$ be a set of edges of $G$ and $H=G/F$. If no layout of $H$ has width
at most $w$, then $\pw(G)\ge w+1$. For every further contraction sequence
on $H$ and every vertex set $S$ of the result, the minimum degree inside $S$
is at most $\pw(G)$; in particular the contraction degeneracy of $H$, which
is the largest minimum degree the minor step reads along its own
contractions, is at most $\pw(G)$~\leanref{tabu}.
\end{theorem}

The second statement is quantified over every sequence because the minor
step does not emit the contractions behind its degeneracy value
(Section~\ref{sec:certs}); each value it reads is a minimum degree of some
contracted copy, and the theorem bounds every such value, so soundness needs
no emitted sequence.

\begin{theorem}[a run reports a floor, and reaches $w$ exactly by a certified
minor]\label{thm:tabu-run}
Model a run as a starting floor $g$ (the program's \texttt{-L} input, trusted
by hypothesis) and a list of visited forests each with a claimed floor
justified by one of the two kinds; the output is the largest claim, or $g$.
Then the output is at most $\pw(G)$; and for $w>g$, the output is at least
$w$ if and only if some visited minor carries a claim of at least $w$, in
which case that minor has pathwidth at least $w$. In particular, if the
output equals the recorded width $w$, some visited minor refuted
$w-1$~\leanref{tabu}.
\end{theorem}

The theorems are stated for an arbitrary list of visited forests: the tabu
rule, the band, the sampling, the aspiration and the seeding decide which
forests are visited and are irrelevant to soundness. One direction is
deliberately not claimed: a visited minor whose pathwidth is $w$ but on which
the engine's budget ran out is not reported, so the hypothesis is the
certified claim and not the bare property; the check below confirmed that the
stronger statement is false. On the MOSP side, a refutation of width $w$ on
a minor of the customer graph proves that every pattern order opens more than
$w+1$ stacks, with no hypothesis on the instance.

\paragraph{Checked before stated.} Every statement was checked by brute
force before it was written: on every graph of the atlas with at most seven
vertices (1,252 graphs) and 15,024 edge subsets, forests and cyclic sets
alike, a quarter in shuffled order, the walk was replayed exactly as the
program prints it, every emitted pair verified to be an edge of the current
graph, the replay verified to equal the quotient, the quotient's pathwidth
computed over all orderings and compared with the graph's (tight on 1,614),
every degeneracy value re-derived from the program's own rule and bounded,
and 3,756 simulated runs compared with the run theorems; no violation
(the script \path{reports/scratch/minor_tabu_lean/check.py}). The axiom audit lists
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound} for every
theorem.

\paragraph{Status.} On five graphs of known pathwidth the search reproduces
the exact value. On the open classes of the dataset the first run, seeded
from each class's best certificate on record, is in progress at the time of
writing; its tally, the classes closed and the floors raised, with every new
certificate passed through the independent checker, will be reported here.
What the search cannot do is what no minor search can: where no minor of the
sizes it visits refutes $w-1$, as on \texttt{Random-125-125-2-2\_0}, the
certificate is the whole graph.
